Grand Ocean Sokhna Awarded 5-Star Status Following Renovation.

New Cairo, Egypt • June 11, 2026 • By The Executive Board

The international tourism board has officially elevated Grand Ocean Sokhna to a five-star rating, marking a historic milestone for the coastal flagship. This recognition follows a comprehensive two-year transformation that modernized the architectural footprint while honoring the site's natural Red Sea topography.

"Our goal was never just to update the furnishings," stated the CEO during the press briefing. "We completely reimagined the spatial flow to create invisible luxury—an intuitive environment where wellness, sustainability, and high-end gastronomy converge perfectly without ever feeling forced upon the guest."

"We are proving that world-class luxury and environmental stewardship are not mutually exclusive."

A Paradigm Shift in Coastal Luxury

The renovation touched every aspect of the resort. The 250 guest rooms were stripped to their studs and rebuilt with locally sourced Egyptian limestone, smart climate control systems, and panoramic floor-to-ceiling windows that erase the boundary between indoors and the azure waters outside.

Moreover, the wellness center was expanded into a 3,000-square-meter sanctuary featuring thalassotherapy pools, salt rooms, and holistic treatment pavilions inspired by ancient Egyptian healing practices. The culinary program was similarly overhauled, introducing two new signature dining venues focused on zero-kilometer seafood sourcing.

Sustainable Operations at the Forefront

Earning the 5-star designation wasn't solely about aesthetics or expanded menus. The international tourism board heavily highlighted Grand Ocean Sokhna's newly implemented green infrastructure as a deciding factor.

A massive solar array installed above the covered parking and utility buildings now powers 40% of the resort's energy needs. Furthermore, a state-of-the-art desalination plant ensures responsible water usage without drawing from local community resources, and the property has completely eliminated single-use plastics from its guest-facing operations.
